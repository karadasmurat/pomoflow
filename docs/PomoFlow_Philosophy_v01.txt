POMOFLOW — THE PHILOSOPHY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────


Focus on what you invest, not what you finish.

Most productivity tools measure output: tasks completed, boxes checked,
deadlines met. PomoFlow measures something different — the effort you put
in, and where it actually went. That shift, small as it sounds, changes
how you relate to your work entirely.


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE CORE INSIGHT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Turning a deadline into an effort budget

"Finish this assignment by Friday" is a binary outcome. It tells you
nothing about how to get there, and it creates mounting anxiety as the
deadline approaches. The moment you miss it, the record reads: failed.

"Give this three hours of honest effort by Friday" is a commitment you
can always keep. You can't always control when a difficult problem is
solved, but you can always control whether you showed up for it. Focus
for an hour, and you're fifty percent done with your intent. That is real
progress — and it compounds.

    "You can't always control when something is finished.
     You can always control whether you gave it honest effort."


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHY IT MATTERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Awareness is the beginning of alignment

Most people don't have a focus problem. They have a visibility problem.
They end the day feeling busy but uncertain whether they moved the things
that actually matter. PomoFlow solves this by binding every timer session
to a named area of work — so your history isn't just a log of minutes,
it's a map of where your energy went.

When you can see that pattern clearly, you can change it. Not through
guilt or pressure, but through simple awareness: is my effort flowing
toward what I care about?


─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW IT WORKS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Four steps. A loop you repeat daily.


01 — DEFINE
Name what matters

Add the areas of work, study, or life that deserve your attention. Give each a color. These aren't just labels — they're a declaration of what you're choosing to invest in.


02 — COMMIT
Set your intent before you start

Before the timer starts, name where this block of time is going. That single choice — made consciously, before the work begins — is where busy becomes purposeful.


03 — FOCUS
Show up fully

Work. Rest. Repeat. Your only job is to be present for the session you committed to. Progress isn't built in marathons — it's built in honest, repeated acts of showing up.


04 — REFLECT
See where your energy went

Review how your effort is flowing across your focus areas — not to judge, but to understand. One honest look is worth more than a week of good intentions.
