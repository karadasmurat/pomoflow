Competitive positioning

What makes PomoFlow
different from everything else

Most productivity tools measure completion. PomoFlow measures investment — and that changes everything about how progress feels.

01
Effort over output — a different measure of progress

Most tools (Todoist, Things, Notion tasks) are built around completion. PomoFlow is built around investment. The core question shifts from "did you finish?" to "did you show up honestly?" — a question you can always answer yes to, even when life gets in the way. This isn't just a philosophy; it shapes every feature decision in the product.

02
Visibility shrinks the shadow of a deadline

PomoFlow frames deadline anxiety as a visibility problem, not a focus problem. Most dread comes from not knowing where you stand — not from the work itself. The Focus Planner doesn't help you go faster. It helps you see clearly. When you can see your effort honestly, the deadline is still there — but its shadow gets smaller.

03
Commitment blocks, not tasks

Instead of to-do items, users place session-based effort commitments on a calendar — "give this 3 hours by Friday." These are distinct from deadline markers (events in the world) and actuals (what really happened). Showing all three layers simultaneously on one calendar is a deliberate design choice that standard task managers and even most calendar apps don't attempt.

04
The immutable past / live future split

A technically distinctive feature: past commitment blocks freeze their duration the moment the day passes (snapshotDuration), while future blocks recalculate live from current session settings. The calendar always tells a true story — you can't accidentally rewrite history by changing your Pomodoro length today. This level of data integrity is unusual in consumer productivity software.

05
Sessions as the unit of planning

Rather than hours or arbitrary blocks, everything is expressed in Pomodoro sessions — the unit the user has already configured. The UI derives durations automatically ("2 sessions = 85 minutes at your settings"), removing mental arithmetic and keeping planning tightly coupled to how you actually work at your desk.

06
Reflect as a first-class step

The four-step loop — Define → Commit → Focus → Reflect — makes review a core product behaviour, not an afterthought. The effort history is framed as "a map of where your energy went" — a diagnostic tool, not a guilt log. The question it poses is simply: is my time reflecting my priorities?

The short version

Where competitors help you manage tasks, PomoFlow helps you manage attention and intention — and it's built a coherent philosophy, data model, and UX all pointed at the same idea.
